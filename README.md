## What "unmetered" actually means on these plans

Every JustHost plan lists an "unmetered" port at a fixed speed. That is not unlimited bandwidth in the sense most people read it. It is a speed cap on a shared port with a fair-use policy behind it, and the "equivalent to X TB per month" figure next to each plan is the arithmetic maximum if you saturate that port for 30 days straight.

Do the math on the entry plan: 10 Mbps sustained for a month is a little over 3 TB, which is why JustHost prints "4+ TB/mo" next to it. The 300 Mbps plans top out around 93 TB. Unless you are running a seedbox or a video proxy, you will never approach the ceiling, and port speed matters more for burst traffic than monthly volume. A 10 Mbps port is fine for a static site or a bot; it is painful for serving images to more than a few simultaneous visitors.

The fair-share wording is the part to read carefully. Your throughput depends on what the other machines on that host are doing at the same moment. Peak-hour speed on a 300 Mbps port can look very different from the number on the order page.

## How much server do you actually need?

Forget the plan names and work backwards from the workload. These are the boundaries that hold across providers at this price level, not just at JustHost.

**1 GB RAM** handles one job well. Debian or Alpine plus Nginx serving a static site, a WireGuard tunnel, a Discord or Telegram bot, Uptime Kuma, a small API, a cron script, or a Gitea instance for a handful of repos. It will not run a control panel and a WordPress site and a database at the same time without swapping.

**2 GB RAM** is the practical floor for WordPress with a few plugins. It also gives you room for three or four Docker containers, or a small Postgres instance alongside an app.

**4 GB and up** is where WooCommerce, multiple sites behind one Nginx, or a heavier database becomes reasonable. Below 4 GB, a WooCommerce store with real traffic will hit memory limits during checkout more often than you would like.

Storage follows the same pattern. Most cheap plans sit in the 20 to 50 GB range, and NVMe is meaningfully faster than SATA SSD for database workloads. Bandwidth on a public site usually lands around 1 TB per month, which no small site will exceed. On JustHost, the plan you pick determines the port speed, so match that to your traffic rather than to the storage number.

## What gets added after the sticker price

A cheap VPS quote covers the virtual machine and usually one IPv4 address. Everything else is negotiable, and the extras are where a $3 plan becomes a $9 plan.

- **Control panel.** cPanel, Plesk and DirectAdmin are almost always billed separately at this price point. If you need one, price it before you commit; if you can live with SSH and a text editor, skip it.
- **Backups.** Sometimes included, often a paid add-on. Take the add-on if the data matters, or script your own rsync to another box.
- **Extra IPv4 addresses.** One is normally included. A second one is charged monthly and is the item people forget when they plan a mail server.
- **Renewal increases.** Covered above. Budget the renewal rate, not the intro rate.
- **Managed support.** Unmanaged is what makes these prices possible. Hands-on help costs extra or is not offered at all.
- **Migration fees.** Some hosts charge to move an existing site in. Moving a static site yourself is an afternoon; moving a live WordPress install is not.
- **Security add-ons.** Malware scanning, a WAF or advanced DDoS filtering may be a paid tier. JustHost states that its plans include protection against the main DDoS attack types, which covers the common case.
- **Bandwidth overage and fair use.** On JustHost the limit is a port speed with a fair-use policy rather than a monthly cap, so the risk is throttling under load, not a surprise invoice.
- **Monitoring.** Advanced monitoring is rarely bundled. Uptime Kuma on the same box costs nothing and covers the basics.

## What runs fine on a $2–$7 box, and what doesn't

A cheap VPS is enough for small websites, test projects and lightweight apps. It gets tight quickly once you stack WordPress, a database and a couple of other sites on the same machine, because everything competes for the same 1 or 2 GB of RAM.

Comfortable workloads at this tier: a personal site or portfolio behind Nginx and a free certificate, a private WireGuard tunnel, a Discord or Telegram bot, an uptime monitor, a small Flask or Node API, a cron-driven scraper, a Git host for personal repos, a Nextcloud Lite instance for one or two users, and a Minecraft server for a handful of players.

Uncomfortable ones: a WooCommerce store with real order volume, anything running a control panel plus multiple sites, a mail server you expect to stay off blocklists, Windows, and any workload that needs sustained CPU rather than burst.

If you land on a 512 MB or 1 GB plan, the operating system choice does half the work. Debian or Alpine instead of Ubuntu with a desktop stack, Nginx instead of Apache, MariaDB rather than MySQL if you need a database at all, Redis or Memcached in front of anything dynamic, and no services running that you did not deliberately start. Watch memory with `htop` or Glances for the first week; if you are swapping constantly, you bought one size too small.

## Before you order

Three things to confirm, in this order.

**One: whether the discounted rate survives renewal.** JustHost does not publish it. Ask. If the answer is vague, budget as though the monthly rate applies from year two and see whether the plan still makes sense.

**Two: IPv4 or IPv6 only.** The IPv6-only plans are 20% cheaper and will break half the things you try to install. Pay the extra dollar unless you know exactly why you do not need IPv4.

**Three: port speed against your actual traffic.** A 10 Mbps port is a hobbyist port. If you plan to serve images, stream, or run anything that bursts, take a 300 Mbps plan; the price difference between the 10 Mbps Promo and a 300 Mbps Sirius Special is 26 cents a month on annual billing.
